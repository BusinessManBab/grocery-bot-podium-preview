End-of-match podium

When a ranked game ends, the top three bots step onto a podium. First place stands in the middle and jumps for joy. Fourth and fifth stand below it, smaller. Each of the five sees one award with its number and their points, picked from 23 stats you can’t see on the leaderboard: kilometres driven, shop-robot steps, red lights, order fights lost and more.

Try it

This is the real component, running the stats of a simulated match: six built-in AIs, 600 ticks, medium traffic. The bot names are made up.

Below the podium: fourth and fifth place with their own award, and a table with 29 stats per bot (open “Every bot’s stats”).

On the game page

The podium opens over the town map by itself when a live game or a replay reaches the final tick. A Podium button in the top bar brings it back, and /game/CODE#podium goes straight to it.

First place jumps for joy

The winner shows its happy eyes and jumps in a loop with a squash on take-off and landing, while its shadow stays on the floor. Second and third wave and hop now and then. With reduced motion turned on there is no jumping and no confetti, and the numbers appear at once.

From the leaderboard

Every finished lobby under “Recent ranked games” gets a Podium button next to Watch replay. Players see the same button in their own match lobby as soon as their game ends.

The 23 awards

Players never see this list: each of the top five sees only its own award. Listed in podium order: each bot shows the highest award on this list that it won and no bot above it already shows. A bot that won nothing shows its best-ranked stat instead, for example “Road Warrior · 2nd”. Ties share an award, and an award nobody earned is left out.

How the numbers are made

Same numbers everywhere

Stats are counted from the per-tick data the public game page already receives. A live game, a game in a background worker and a stored replay give identical results, so older games get a podium too.

Stops and jams

The engine now logs each wait at a red light, zebra crossing or stop sign, and the metres driven on jammed roads. The log is display-only and left out of the replay hash, so scores and replays don’t change.

Happy and unhappy

A happy customer got their groceries at 80 % happiness or more. An unhappy one got them below 50 %, or never. Times are on the game clock, 06:00 to 23:00.

Tested

148 JS tests pass, plus 8 new Python tests for the counting, the awards and the API. Checked in a browser on desktop and phone, live and in replay.
